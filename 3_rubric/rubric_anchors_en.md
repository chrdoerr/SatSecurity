# Assessment rubric: anchors of Table 4

One level per area and organization, assigned by best fit to the anchors (Section 4.3). Expected targeting forms categories, not an ordinal scale: reckons with an attempt within the year (4, 5), deems one possible (3), or not (1, 2); 0 = no estimate (question not asked or not answered).

## Knowledge of the threat landscape (RQ2)

- **5** — **Beyond own area.** Threat types beyond own area of responsibility; concrete attack vectors and attacker groups; past incidents; knows which institutions work on counterspace; engages with attacker motivation
- **4** — **Own area.** Broad knowledge of the threat types affecting own area of responsibility; damage potential of selected types; attack vectors in that area
- **3** — **Single types.** Names individual threat types affecting own area unprompted; thinks of state attackers, and of military missions as the targets
- **2** — **In principle.** Knows that systems in space can be attacked, but no concrete threat type that touches own area
- **1** — **None.** No knowledge of, and no understanding of, counterspace threats

## Information acquisition (RQ2)

- **5** — **Systematic.** Broad self-initiated acquisition, national and international, from diverse sources; actively seeks cooperation partners; acquisition is part of the work
- **4** — **Regular exchange.** Regular active exchange through events, exercises or customer contact; wants more information and will spend resources on it
- **3** — **Occasional.** Small-scale active acquisition; regular light input from news or a trade fair; consults individual national or international bodies
- **2** — **Passive.** Passive exposure only — incidental at events or in the news; anecdotal, long ago
- **1** — **None.** No acquisition and no input; little or no interest

## Risk practice (RQ3)

- **5** — **Continuous.** Standardised, continuous, exploratory risk capture across the whole area of responsibility; invests in protection; raises security with customers unprompted
- **4** — **Selective.** Continuous methods for selective risk capture; risk acceptance is an active decision; protection only partly understood as part of the product
- **3** — **One-off.** A one-off analysis of selected threats; isolated measures on own initiative; knows what could be done
- **2** — **Discussed once.** Discussed once within the team; only unintentional hazards are considered in a structured way; responsibility seen as external
- **1** — **None.** Threats are not considered; protection has never been raised; no risk methodology envisaged

## Expected targeting of own mission

- **5** — **Destructive attacks.** Reckons with an attack in the coming year *including* destructive types (ASAT, grappling)
- **4** — **Non-kinetic attacks.** Reckons with an attack in the coming year at least by non-kinetic means (disruption)
- **3** — **Minor impairment.** An attempt causing minor impairment (jamming, information espionage) could occur in the coming year
- **2** — **Distant future.** Isolated threats are relevant, but no attempt expected within the year — rather in the distant future
- **1** — **Irrelevant.** Threats regarded as fundamentally unlikely and not relevant
